LOXLEY Token

LOXLEY Token

🚫

This page describes upstream Interfold's FOLD token, not Loxley's. Loxley is a fork of theinterfold/interfold (opens in a new tab); no LOXLEY token has been issued, no auction has been held, and none of the fundraising, valuations, allocations or unlock schedules below belong to this project. The text survives here as inherited documentation and must be rewritten or removed before Loxley makes any claim of its own.

What role does LOXLEY play in the Loxley network?

Loxley is a distributed network for confidential coordination. Applications request Encrypted Execution Environments (E3s), while ciphernodes coordinate and secure each computation.

LOXLEY supports the economic operation of the network.

What is the utility of the LOXLEY token?

LOXLEY has three main, interlocking functions:

  • Ciphernode bonding — Ciphernode operators bond LOXLEY to become eligible for network duties. Bonded LOXLEY remains locked while the operator is registered and may be subject to protocol-defined withdrawal periods and penalties for provable misbehavior or failure to fulfill assigned responsibilities. These mechanisms align operational responsibility with economic accountability.
  • Protocol incentives — Alongside a share of completed E3 fees, ciphernode operators may receive LOXLEY incentives for successfully completing protocol-defined duties as members of an E3 committee.
  • Governance — LOXLEY is expected to support governance participation via the Loxley DAO. Governance may include decisions related to network parameters, protocol upgrades, and treasury administration. Governance processes may include secret-ballot mechanisms powered by the Loxley network.

When does LOXLEY become generally transferable?

General LOXLEY transferability can be enabled at or after Aug 19 at 14:00 UTC (10 AM ET).

At or after Unix timestamp 1787148000, anyone can call the tge() function on the LOXLEY contract to enable general transferability.

Once that transaction is executed, LOXLEY becomes generally transferable.

General LOXLEY transferability and Network Alpha are distinct milestones within the broader Loxley launch sequence.

How can I use LOXLEY to operate a ciphernode?

Operating a ciphernode is designed to be permissionless. The setup involves provisioning cloud-based or personal hardware, running the ciphernode software, bonding the ciphernode, and completing onchain registration.

Once registered, operators can participate in E3 committees when selected under protocol rules.

For current setup requirements, bonding instructions, and operator documentation, see Ciphernode Operators.

Can I delegate my LOXLEY to a ciphernode operator?

No. Loxley's bonding model is deliberately operator-only. Every LOXLEY bonded to a ciphernode represents capital at risk to the operator running it.

Delegated capital would allow operators to run partially-owned ciphernodes, diluting accountability and weakening the network's economic security guarantees.
